5 Proven Strategies for Women to Stop Self-Censoring in High-Stakes Meetings

By Erim Kaur · 4 min read

Almost every female client I work with has a version of the same story. They had something to say in the meeting. They didn't say it. Someone else did, often less well — and got credit for it. Self-censorship isn't a character flaw. It's a trained response. Here are five strategies to untrain it.

1. Speak in the first ten minutes

Research from MIT shows the longer you wait to speak in a meeting, the less likely you are to speak at all. Set yourself a rule: contribute something in the first ten minutes. A clarifying question counts. A "yes, and" counts. The hardest sentence is the first one. After that, you're a participant, not an audience.

2. Cut the disclaimer

"I might be wrong, but..." "This is probably a silly question, but..." "Sorry, just to add..." These openers tell the room to discount what comes next before they've heard it. Strip them. Start with the point. If you're wrong, the room will tell you. You don't need to pre-apologise for taking up airtime.

3. Hold your own interruption

The next time someone talks over you, don't go quiet. Two options: (1) Calmly say, "Let me finish that thought." (2) Wait three beats — eye contact held — then continue exactly where you left off. Both work. Both signal: my words deserve to land before yours do.

4. Reclaim your idea — out loud

If a colleague restates your point and the room latches onto their version, name it. "Yes, that builds on the point I made earlier — and what I'd add is..." It is not impolite. It is precise. Precision is leadership.

5. Decide before the meeting starts

Self-censoring is a real-time decision made by a tired brain. Pre-decide. Before the meeting, write down: "I will say one thing about X." Now you're not deciding whether to speak — you're delivering on a commitment to yourself. Much easier to honour.

Your voice doesn't need permission to be in the room. It just needs practice being heard. Start small, start this week — and notice how the room recalibrates around you.
