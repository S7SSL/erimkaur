Beyond the Business Card: 3 Follow-up Secrets to Turn a 5-Minute Chat into a Long-Term Lead

By Erim Kaur · 3 min read

Most networking dies in the 48 hours after the event. You meet someone interesting. You promise to follow up. You don't. They don't. The connection evaporates. Here's the three-part follow-up framework I built while securing partnerships with brands like Pandora, Sky, and HSBC.

Secret #1: The 24-hour rule

Send your follow-up within 24 hours. Not three days. Not a week. Twenty-four hours, while you're still fresh in their inbox and their memory. The email itself doesn't need to be long — three sentences will do — but it must reference one specific thing you actually talked about. "Great to meet you" is forgettable. "Loved your point about X — sent the article we discussed" is unforgettable.

Template: "Hi [Name], really enjoyed our chat about [specific topic] last night. As promised, here's [the link / intro / article]. Would love to keep the conversation going — let me know if a coffee in the next two weeks works."

Secret #2: Give before you ask

The follow-ups that convert are the ones that lead with value. Send a relevant article. Make a useful introduction. Share a podcast you mentioned. Anything that says "I was paying attention to what you need." If your first three touchpoints all give before they ask, your fourth (the actual ask) lands as a favour they want to return — not a cold pitch.

Secret #3: Build a 30-day cadence

The second follow-up is where most relationships go cold. Your first email got a reply, you said "let's stay in touch," and... nothing. Set a calendar reminder for 30 days out. Send something light: a relevant link, congratulations on a win you saw on LinkedIn, a "thinking of you because X" note. Three of these over three months and you're in their orbit. That's where opportunities live — in the orbit, not the inbox.

Why this works

People remember how you made them feel. The follow-up converts the chat into a relationship by signalling: I listened, I valued your time, and I'm here without an agenda. Those three signals are rare. Send them, and you become rare too.

The five-minute conversation isn't the deal. The follow-up is the deal.
