From "I'm Sorry" to "I'm Certain": How to Eliminate Low-Confidence Language

By Erim Kaur · 3 min read

The fastest way to upgrade how you're perceived isn't a new wardrobe, a new title, or a new degree. It's the language you use in the next ten emails you send. Here are five phrases that quietly shrink your authority — and what to say instead.

1. "Sorry to bother you" → "Quick question for you"

You're not bothering anyone. You're communicating, which is your job. Apologising for taking up space tells the reader your message is low-priority before they've read it. They will treat it accordingly. Lead with the question.

2. "Just" → (delete)

"I just wanted to follow up." "I just had a quick thought." "Just checking in." Just is a softener that exists to make you sound less demanding. It does — and it also makes you sound less serious. Search your last week of emails. Delete every "just." Your messages will sound dramatically more confident overnight.

3. "I think maybe..." → "I recommend..."

"I think maybe we should consider going with option B" carries roughly zero conviction. Compare it to: "I recommend option B because..." You're being paid for your judgement. Lead with it.

4. "Does that make sense?" → "What questions do you have?"

"Does that make sense?" subtly puts the burden of clarity on you and invites the listener to nod even if they didn't follow. "What questions do you have?" assumes competence on both sides — and produces actual questions, which produce actual alignment.

5. "I'm no expert, but..." → (delete the disclaimer)

If you weren't qualified to have a view, you wouldn't be in the meeting. Pre-discounting your own opinion is a form of permission-seeking. State the view. If you're wrong, that's information. If you're right, you've just shown leadership.

The 24-hour audit

For the next 24 hours, listen for these phrases in your own speech and writing. You'll be surprised. Most clients notice ten to fifteen instances in a single afternoon. Catch them, swap them, move on. No self-criticism — just calibration.

Confident language doesn't mean louder. It means clearer. Clean up the softeners and watch how differently the room responds.
