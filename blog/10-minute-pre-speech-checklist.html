The 10-Minute Pre-Speech Checklist: From Soundcheck to Self-Talk

By Erim Kaur · 3 min read

The hour before a talk is where most speakers lose. They re-read their notes, second-guess their opening, and let their nervous system spiral. Here's the 10-minute routine I use before every keynote — built from years of getting it wrong before I got it right.

Minutes 0-2: The room

Walk the stage if you can. Stand where you'll be standing. Look out at where the audience will sit. This sounds small — it isn't. Familiarity drops cortisol. By the time the room fills, your body already feels like it has been there before.

Minutes 2-4: Soundcheck

Test the mic. Test the clicker. Find where your slides advance and where your laptop sits. Ask the AV person their name. The single most distracting thing on stage is technology you've never touched. Eliminate that variable now, not in front of 500 people.

Minutes 4-6: Body

Two minutes of an open, expansive posture — hands on hips, shoulders back, chin level. Stretch your jaw. Roll your shoulders three times. Drop your chin to your chest, then lift it. You're not "warming up." You're telling your body the threat scan is over.

Minutes 6-8: Voice

Hum. Yes — actually hum, somewhere private. Hum up and down your vocal range. Then say your opening line out loud, three times, at the volume you'll use on stage. Cold voices crack. Warm voices land.

Minutes 8-9: Self-talk

One sentence, said quietly to yourself: "I'm excited to share this with them." Not "I'm not nervous." Not "I've got this." The reframe that works is the one that turns the body's signal into the body's permission.

Minute 9-10: First sentence

Picture the very first sentence. See yourself say it. See one person nod. That's it. Don't rehearse the whole talk in your head — that's a recipe for blanking. Just the opening.

Then walk out. The work is done. Your only job now is to be the person you've already practiced being. Trust the prep.
